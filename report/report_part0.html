Formula 1 Podium Prediction

For every driver on the starting grid of a race, predict whether they finish in the top three, using only information known after qualifying and before the start.

Summary

  • Data. 26,759 raw result rows become 24,715 driver-race rows: the cars on the grid at the prediction cutoff, one row per driver per race, with the Indianapolis 500 and failed qualifiers removed. Models are trained on every season from 1950 to 2019, tuned on 2020–2021 and tested once on 2022–2024. A later start was tested: none predicts better, and starting in 1994 is significantly worse.
  • Q1, front-row conversion. Front-row cars podiumed more often after 2014: 68.0% → 75.9% (p = 0.013). Suzuka, Yas Marina and Monaco convert best after 2014, but circuits are not statistically distinguishable in either period, so a real change in the ranking cannot be shown. A front-row qualifier moved back by a penalty podiums only 28%, against 73% for one who starts there.
  • Q2, home advantage. Comparing drivers who started from similar grid slots in the same era, home drivers podium 0.8 percentage points less often, and the logistic odds ratio is 0.93 (95% CI 0.79–1.11). There is no evidence of a home advantage.
  • Q3, mechanical retirements (teams grouped by lineage). They fell from 8.9% to 5.5% of race starts. Highest in 2014–2021: Caterham (21.2%), Toro Rosso → AlphaTauri → RB (14.4%), Lotus F1 → Renault → Alpine (13.2%). In 2022–2024 the Enstone team (Alpine, 10.4%) is highest, but team differences are not significant.
  • Models. Five learned models on 18 features reach validation PR-AUC 0.72–0.74, against 0.64 for a grid-only rule and 0.15 for a random model. None beats the logistic-regression baseline by more than noise, so it is the recommended model: test PR-AUC 0.722 (tied best), ROC-AUC 0.937, well calibrated. All five are explained and have their own inference function.
  • Explainability. In all five models, starting position dominates, followed by season-long strength (standings) and recent form. These explanations describe what the models rely on, not what causes a podium.
