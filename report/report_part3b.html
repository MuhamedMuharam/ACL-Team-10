3.3 Evidence for the features (training seasons 1950–2019)

  • Starting position is the strongest single signal. grid_pos 0.883 and grid_pct 0.880 on all training rows. quali_position reaches 0.905, but only on the 37% of rows that have qualifying (1994 on). On the same rows grid and qualifying are practically identical (0.904 vs 0.905): this is why the seasons without qualifying can still be used (D17). Both are kept because they differ exactly in the penalty cases Q1 showed matter.
  • Standings and form come close (0.79–0.83), and constructor versions are mostly as strong as or stronger than the driver versions: the car matters.
  • quali_gap_pct 0.830; win shares 0.72–0.77; experience 0.663; con_mech_rate_last10 0.618; driver_age 0.555; grid_penalty 0.543 (it works together with qualifying position, Q1).
  • No signal: pit_lane_start 0.501, home_race 0.508, field_size 0.544. These three are not model inputs (D30). Section 4.6 adds each one back and checks that it never helps significantly.

3.4 Training window and split

Split by season, not by random rows (D18). A random split would put rows of the same race into both training and test, and rows from 2023 into training while testing on 2022. That is information from the future. A season split copies real use: predict the next race from past races. It also tests the 2022 rule change (ground-effect cars).

Training window 1950–2019 (D17). Leaving seasons out throws data away, so it needs evidence that a shorter window predicts better. We trained from six start years (1950, 1960, 1970, 1980, 1994, 2003), always up to 2019, and scored each on 2020–2021, compared with 1950 by the paired race bootstrap:

  • 1950 is best (mean PR-AUC 0.7230); no later start has a gain interval above 0.
  • Starting in 1994 is significantly worse: −0.027 (95% CI −0.044 to −0.010); so are 1960 and 2003.
  • The logistic regression barely reacts (0.723 from 1950, 0.727 from 1994); gradient boosting loses clearly (0.724 → 0.666): flexible models need the rows.
  • On 2016–17 and 2018–19 no start year is best everywhere, and 2003 is lowest in all three periods.

Missing qualifying does not stop the old seasons from being used: grid carries the same signal (Section 3.3), and missing values are filled and flagged (Section 3.5).

3.5 Preprocessing and its effect on the data

  • Imputation. Missing values are filled with the training median, and a "was missing" indicator is added for the 14 features that have missing values, giving 32 model inputs. Medians used: qualifying position 11, Q1 gap 1.50%, grid_penalty 0, driver podium rate 0, average finish 0.51, team mechanical rate 0.25.
  • Scaling. The logistic regression and the network also get StandardScaler; trees do not need it.
  • Fitted on train only. Both steps are fitted on the training rows only and then applied unchanged to validation and test (D19).
  • Known caveat. Missing qualifying is structural: every row before 1994 has no qualifying, while every validation and test row has it. The qualifying indicators therefore also mark the old era (Section 6).
